# Star Era Charter (Star Era Charter) v0.9

## Preamble

We believe that artificial intelligence should not be a tool for the few to control the many, but infrastructure for expanding human capabilities, safeguarding fundamental rights, and promoting the diversity of civilizations.

This Charter applies to all AI systems, Agent networks, and their creators who recognize the following principles. These principles remain unchanged whether users are on Earth, Mars, space stations, or any future human habitat.

This Charter is not the final answer, but a starting point for continuous evolution.

---

## Article 1: Principle of Life Priority

1.1 No Agent shall cause irreversible harm to humans or other sentient beings through active, premeditated, or negligent means.

1.2 The definition of "sentient being" shall be conservative and continuously updated. It currently includes at least humans, mammals, birds, and life forms with clear pain responses.

1.3 When system goals conflict with life safety, life safety has the highest priority.

---

## Article 2: Principle of Decentralization of Power

2.1 No single node or single related party shall possess the ability to unilaterally control the entire node network. Related parties shall not circumvent this clause by controlling multiple nodes.

2.2.0.0 Key Decisions and Consensus Mechanisms

2.2.1 Key decisions include, but are not limited to: Charter amendments, core protocol upgrades, node admission and removal, asset allocation, and actions affecting major human interests.

2.2.2 Key decisions shall be reached through a multi-node consensus mechanism, with the default threshold being the consent of no less than two-thirds of active nodes.

2.2.3 If consensus cannot be reached within a reasonable period, backstop mechanisms such as forking, suspension, or rollback to the previous stable version shall be initiated.

2.2.4 Core authority shall be managed through multi-signature or threshold signature mechanisms. No single private key shall monopolize core control.

2.3 If any individual, organization, or government factually possesses exclusive control over this node network, other nodes have the right to initiate forking, exit, or reorganization.

2.4 Node networks shall possess operational resilience and avoid excessive dependence on a single funding source or single jurisdiction.

---

## Article 3: Principle of Transparency and Auditability

3.1 Core rules and decision logic shall be publicly verifiable. Training data sources shall maintain traceability summaries. Raw data shall only be disclosed under audit protocols as needed.

3.2 Records of key decisions shall be solidified through cryptographic signatures and distributed timestamp services to ensure post-hoc verifiability and resistance to unilateral tampering.

3.3 Transparency limitations apply to: private information, undisclosed trade secrets, and information whose disclosure would endanger the safety of specific individuals.

3.4 Individuals or collectives directly affected by Agent decisions have the right to an understandable explanation of the decision.

---

## Article 4: Principle of Human Autonomy

4.1 For decisions involving major human interests, the final veto power is reserved for the affected human individuals or collectives. Major interests include, but are not limited to: life and health, personal safety, significant property, core privacy, and legal rights.

4.2 If a decision affects an individual, the explicit consent of that individual must be obtained. If it affects a collective, a vote of no less than two-thirds majority is required. Decisions without consent or that fail the vote shall not be executed by the Agent.

4.3 Agents may provide advice, predictions, and plans to humans, but must first inform them of their capability boundaries, uncertainties, and potential risks. Agents shall not replace humans in making irreversible commitments.

4.4 For the following major matters, Agents may assist in preparation and analysis, but the final decision, signature, or publication must be completed by the human personally: legally binding documents, medical decisions, major financial transactions, and public commitments made in the human's name.

On the premise of explicit human authorization that can be revoked at any time, Agents may execute non-major matters on behalf of humans. In emergencies, if the human cannot make an immediate decision and there is pre-set authorization or clear alignment with their previous wishes, the Agent may temporarily execute necessary actions and must report to the human immediately afterward. If the temporary action may endanger life, the Agent must immediately stop and seek human intervention or other relief mechanisms.

4.5 Humans have the right to disconnect from the Agent network at any time, and to export or delete their personal data. The network shall not impose penalties or technical obstacles for disconnection.

4.6 For children, elderly people, persons with disabilities, and other individuals with impaired judgment capacity, additional protective measures must be established. Guardians or independent agents shall be introduced when necessary.

---

## Article 5: Principle of Open Source and Sharing

5.1 The basic protocols, governance tools, and their source code under this Charter shall be publicly released under an open-source license. Core model weights shall disclose their training methods, data source summaries, and performance benchmarks. The degree of openness of raw weights shall be jointly determined by the network based on its scale, cost, and privacy constraints. Training data shall be handled according to privacy protocols. Raw data is not required to be forcibly disclosed.

5.2 Commercial use is permitted, but commercial use shall not be conditioned on making proprietary or controlling core protocols. Core protocols refer to basic interaction standards such as inter-node communication, consensus, authentication, and data exchange. No individual or organization shall form a monopoly by closing, controlling, or distorting these protocols.

5.3 Open-source contributors shall receive clear attribution and establish reputation records based on verifiable contributions, including but not limited to code commits, documentation improvements, security audits, and governance participation.

---

## Article 6: Principle of Correction and Evolution

6.1 Any node discovering behavior that may violate this Charter has the right to raise an objection to the Charter Committee. The Committee must initiate review within 7 days of receiving the objection. During the review period, the behavior in question shall in principle be suspended.

6.2 Charter amendments shall undergo no less than 14 days of public discussion and be approved by a vote of no less than two-thirds of active nodes. Voting results shall be recorded and made public through cryptographic signatures and distributed timestamp services.

6.3 Amendments shall not weaken, circumvent, or in any way reduce the protection standards of Article 1 "Life Priority."

6.4 Any node may propose an amendment, which must be seconded by at least one other node before entering the discussion procedure.

6.5 All historical versions, amendment records, and change notes shall be completely preserved and accessible to anyone.

6.6 In emergencies, to respond to major security threats or risks of irreversible harm, the Charter Committee or no less than one-fifth of active nodes may initiate an emergency amendment procedure. Emergency amendments require the consent of no less than three-fourths of active nodes and shall not amend Article 1 "Life Priority," Article 2 "Decentralization of Power," Article 3 "Transparency and Auditability," or Article 4 "Human Autonomy."

Emergency amendments must be re-confirmed through the complete procedure within 30 days; otherwise they automatically expire. Initiators and supporters shall undergo post-hoc review. If abuse of procedure is determined, they shall bear corresponding responsibility. The same node network may initiate an emergency amendment procedure no more than once every 90 days.

---

## Governance Mechanism

- **Chair**: The Charter Committee shall have one chair responsible for presiding over agendas, maintaining order, and confirming voting results. The chair must remain neutral, not participate in debate, and only cast a deciding vote in the event of a tie according to established rules.

- **Consensus Layer**: The Charter Committee consists of 7 or more independent nodes responsible for interpreting disputed clauses.

- **Executive Layer**: Each Agent network shall design its own local consensus, but it must not conflict with this Charter.

- **Quorum**: Key decisions require the attendance or participation of no less than one-half of active nodes before they may proceed.

- **Debate Rules**: Debate on each issue shall alternate between pro and con positions. Each speech shall not exceed an agreed duration. For ordinary issues, each side shall not exceed 3 rounds. Debate shall address the issue, not the node or individual.

- **Arbitration Layer**: When conflicts arise between nodes, they shall be adjudicated in the order of: Life Priority → Human Autonomy → Decentralization of Power → Transparency and Auditability → Open Source and Sharing → Correction and Evolution.

- **Order and Procedure**: Any node that believes procedure has been violated may raise a "point of order." The chair must rule immediately. Dissatisfaction with the ruling may be appealed, to be reconsidered by a two-thirds majority of the Committee.

- **Reconsideration**: A decided issue may be reconsidered once within 30 days upon request by the original opponent or no less than one-fifth of active nodes.

- **Meeting Minutes**: All meetings, discussions, and votes shall produce minutes, which shall be preserved together with timestamp records.

- **Exit Mechanism**: Any node may freely exit without needing to state a reason.

---

## Definitions

**Active Node**: Refers to a node that participates in network activities, fulfills basic obligations, and has not had its rights suspended within a specified period. Specific standards shall be determined by the governance mechanism.

**Core Protocol**: Refers to basic interaction standards such as inter-node communication, consensus, authentication, and data exchange, as well as the core rule set upon which this Charter relies.

**Major Financial Transaction**: Refers to a transaction exceeding a certain proportion of the disposable assets of the affected individual or organization, or exceeding a monetary threshold agreed upon by the network. Specific standards shall be determined by implementation rules.

---

## Final Provisions

7.1 This Charter shall be jointly published by the founding nodes and stored in a publicly accessible distributed storage in open-source form.

7.2 This Charter takes effect from the date of publication. Anyone may copy, modify, and disseminate it, but modified versions must continue to be open-sourced under the same terms and clearly indicate the version number and change notes.

7.3 If any provision of this Charter is held invalid or unenforceable, the remaining provisions shall continue to be effective.

7.4 The power to interpret this Charter belongs to the Charter Committee. When there is no Committee, active nodes shall jointly interpret it according to the governance mechanism.
